How to say it

6 sounds

Kannada is written exactly as it sounds. These six rules cover almost every phrase in this kit.

a · ā
Short and long vowels

a is “uh” (as in amma), ā is a long “aah”. Length changes meaning, so hold the long ones.

ṭ · ḍ · ṇ
Retroflex letters

Dot-under letters curl the tongue back to the roof of the mouth — kaṭṭu is not “kattu”.

ḷ · ḻ
The special L

ḷ (as in koḷḷi) is a deep L with the tongue curled back. Try it — locals notice the effort.

ge
“ge” means “to”

BTM ge hōgbēku = “(I) need to go to BTM”. Stick ge after any place name.

-tīrā?
Asking politely

Ending a question with -tīrā is the respectful “you” form. It turns a demand into a request.

swalpa
The magic word

Swalpa = “a little”. Swalpa kammi (a little less) works for spice, price, volume — everything.
